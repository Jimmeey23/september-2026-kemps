Renewal has fallen 15.9 points in twelve months and the studio has no recovery mechanism
Observation
The paid-membership renewal rate fell from 75.7% in October 2025 to 59.8% in September 2026 — the lowest month in the series and the fifth consecutive month below 66%.
Evidence
Monthly resolved renewal rates: Oct-25 75.7% · Nov 72.1% · Dec 75.5% · Jan-26 75.3% · Feb 75.2% · Mar 72.1% · Apr 74.4% · May 69.7% · Jun 63.1% · Jul 65.0% · Aug 62.2% · Sep 59.8%. Expiry volume rose from 181 to 189 over the same period, so absolute lapses rose from 44 to 70 (+59%).
Interpretation
The decline is real and it is not a volume artefact. Two things changed: more memberships are reaching expiry (the base grew) and a smaller share of them are being renewed. The break is visible after April 2026 — the seven months to April average 74.3%, the five months since average 64.0%.
Why it matters
At the September rate, the 100 active memberships expiring in the next 30 days will produce roughly 40 lapses. At the observed mean lapsed value of ₹11,738 that is about ₹470,000 of membership value in a single month — before counting the ongoing spend those members would have made.
Action
Treat renewal as a managed pipeline, not an event. Assign the 100 memberships expiring within 30 days to named associates this week, working the list in order of sessions completed (the strongest predictor available) rather than by expiry date.
Expected outcome
Renewal rate stabilising and then recovering; lapses converted into renewal conversations before they happen.
Success metric
Renewal rate from 59.8% to 68% within two quarters; 100% of 30-day expiries assigned to a named associate.

The five numbers that should drive October

  • 59.8% — September renewal rate versus 75.7% a year ago. A 15.9-point decline.
  • 87.0% vs 50.9% — renewal rate for members completing 11+ sessions versus 1–4 sessions (n=2,087). Consumption is the lever.
  • 0 of 70 — September lapses that repurchased a membership or package by 10 October. Gross churn is net churn.
  • 100 of 208 — active memberships expiring within 30 days, carrying ₹1,741,709 of value.
  • 30 of 208 — memberships with a card on file. Autorenew cannot be switched on for the other 178.

The intro offer converts one in six — it is acquiring trial, not members
Observation
Six Newcomers 2 For 1 memberships expired in September. One renewed: 16.7%, against 59.8% for the cohort as a whole.
Evidence

Interpretation

Why it matters
If acquisition is measured on units sold, the intro offer looks successful. Measured on members retained, it is the weakest product in the book. The studio is buying trial volume that largely does not convert.
Action
Re-scope the intro offer around a defined conversion path. Require a 30-day, 6-visit activation target for every intro purchase, assign each to a named associate, and measure the offer on 60-day conversion to a paid membership rather than on units sold.
Expected outcome
Intro offers converted into members rather than into single visits.
Success metric
60-day intro-to-membership conversion reported and raised from roughly 17% to above 35%.

The Consumption Finding

Across 2,087 paid memberships over twelve months, one variable separates renewers from lappers more sharply than any other: how much of the membership they actually used.

Renewal rate by sessions completed during the membership

Twelve-month paid cohort, n=2,087, base renewal rate 69.0%. Sample sizes: 798 / 338 / 408 / 543. The 2–4 session band sits below the 0–1 band because the 0–1 group is dominated by single-class purchases, which renew on a different logic.

A: 11+ sessions completed7368.5%

September's rates are 14–18 points below the twelve-month average in every band. The band ordering holds perfectly: A 68.5% > B 53.7% > D 45.6% > C 33.3%. September was worse across the board, not worse in one segment.

A member who has used their membership eleven times renews at 87%. One who used it twice renews at 51%.
Observation
Consumption during the membership is the strongest single predictor of renewal available in the data. The spread between the top and bottom band is 36.1 percentage points.
Evidence
Twelve-month paid cohort (n=2,087, base 69.0%): 11+ sessions completed → 87.0% (n=543); 5–10 → 72.8% (n=408); 2–4 → 50.9% (n=338); 0–1 → 62.7% (n=798). Sessions used percentage shows the same shape: 91.7–100% used → 81.7% (n=263) against ≤75% used → 48.2% (n=224).
Interpretation
This is not a tautology. Sessions completed is measured during the membership, before the renewal decision; it is not a record of the renewal itself. The 0–1 band breaks the pattern because it is mostly single-class drop-ins whose renewal decision follows different logic. Within the products that carry a term — unlimiteds and multi-class packages — the relationship is close to monotonic.
Why it matters
Renewal is decided in the first third of the membership, not in the last week. A member who has attended twice by the halfway point is unlikely to renew whatever the front desk says in week 11. The intervention window is early.
Action
Trigger on consumption, not on calendar. Set a day-21 review for every membership: under 3 sessions by day 21 of an unlimited, under 2 by day 21 of a package → named-associate call and a booked next visit before they leave the conversation.
Expected outcome
Members moved from the 2–4 band toward the 5–10 band before the decision point.
Success metric
Share of expiring memberships in the 5+ session band rising from 60% to 75%; renewal rate from 59.8% to 68%.

Usable — measured at or before expiry

  • Total Sessions Completed — 50.9% → 87.0%. The strongest signal.
  • Sessions Used % — 48.2% → 81.7%. Same construct, expressed as a rate.
  • Average Sessions Per Month — 61.2% → 83.1%. Monotonic and clean.
  • Revenue Per Session — 81.2% → 50.8%. Inverted, and meaningful: members on high per-visit prices (drop-ins, privates) renew far worse than members on amortised unlimited pricing.

Confounded — do not act on these alone

  • Late Cancellations — members with 2+ late cancels renew at 82.3% against 65.7% for those with 0–1. This reverses the intuitive reading. It is a volume artefact: members who book frequently both cancel more often and renew more often. Report 08 shows the same pattern.
  • Cancellation Rate % — non-monotonic (65.3% / 78.1% / 67.4%). The middle band is highest. No usable signal.
  • Membership Duration — the 7–26 day band renews worst at 51.6%. This is the short-package effect in §4, not an independent duration effect.

Three fields in this file are leaky and must not be used for prediction

The lapsed file carries Days Since Last Visit, Visits in Last 30 Days and Attendance Decline Rate %. All three are computed as at the report date (October 2026), not as at the membership's end date. For a membership that expired in October 2025, "visits in last 30 days" is measured eleven months after the renewal decision was made.

This produces results that look meaningful and are not. Members with 6+ visits in the last 30 days appear to renew at 86.1% against 62.2% for those with 0–1 — but those visits largely happened after the membership ended. The apparent signal is partly the outcome leaking backwards into the predictor.

None of the three is used anywhere in this report. Every driver in §6 and every coefficient in §7 comes from a field measured at or before expiry. Any model built on the three leaky fields will appear accurate in backtesting and will fail in use.

The studio already has a risk field, and it disagrees with the studio's own data
Observation
The Day End Report – Part 5 – Active file carries a pre-computed RISK LEVEL for each active membership. On the studio's own recency measure, the LOW RISK group is the least recently active of all four bands.
Evidence
Mean days since last activity by risk level: NO RISK 8.9 (n=76) · MEDIUM RISK 21.1 (n=30) · HIGH RISK 24.7 (n=71) · LOW RISK 37.8 (n=31). LOW RISK members have been away 53% longer than HIGH RISK members. Utilisation follows no order either: NO RISK 45.2%, HIGH RISK 60.6%, MEDIUM RISK 68.6%, LOW RISK 70.0%.
Interpretation
The field is not measuring what its name implies. It appears to blend product type, membership progress and attendance cadence into a label whose ordering does not correspond to churn risk. It may still be useful as a workflow prompt, but it cannot be used to prioritise.
Why it matters
Front-desk staff acting on this field will contact LOW RISK members last — precisely the group that has been away longest. The tool is inverting the priority order.
Action
Replace risk level with days-since-last-visit plus sessions completed as the sorting key on every renewal list. Both fields are already in the file and both are measured correctly.
Expected outcome
Renewal outreach reaching the members most likely to lapse first.
Success metric
Renewal lists sorted on recency + consumption; RISK LEVEL retained only as a secondary note.

The Auto Renew field reads NO for all 208 active Kwality House memberships. Only 30 (14.4%) have a Stripe payment token on file. 115 are settled as "custom", 31 in cash and 32 across multiple methods.

Why it matters
Every renewal requires a member to walk in or call and a staff member to be present to take it. That is 208 conversations a month that must go right, in a month where 40% of them are going wrong. Action
Run a card-capture programme before an autorenew programme. Target: card on file for 60% of active memberships within two quarters, starting with the 30-day expiry list where the renewal conversation is already happening. Only then switch autorenew on, and only for members in consumption Bands A and B.
Expected outcome
A growing share of renewals processed automatically, and staff time redirected to the members who are genuinely at risk.
Success metric
Card-on-file coverage from 14.4% to 60%; autorenew live for Band A and B members with ≥30% opt-out monitored.

A1. Assign and work the 30-day expiry list this week

Methodology & Limitations

Cohort definition, source handling, and the three fields that were deliberately excluded.

Sources and cohort definition

  • Primary: Lapsed New – Lapsed (20).csv — 29,148 membership records, 53 columns. 11,710 for Kwality House on Primary Location.
  • Forward view: Day End Report – Part 5 – Active (4).csv — 578 currently-active memberships, 74 columns. 208 for Kwality House on Home Location.
  • Outcome check: Sales – Raw – Sales (23).csv, used to test whether lapsed members repurchased.
  • Cohort: paid memberships (Amount Paid > 0) whose End Date falls in the month, End Date before 1 Oct 2026. Free, complementary, staff and referral products are excluded from every renewal rate because they carry no renewal decision.
  • Renewal rate: renewals ÷ (renewals + lapses). Frozen memberships are excluded from the denominator — no decision has been reached.

Reconciliation and known data faults

  • Status and Derived Status disagree systematically and must not be interchanged. Of 18,880 records marked Status = Renewed, 18,039 carry Derived Status = Lapsed. Derived Status is the lifecycle truth; Status is the per-membership label. This report uses Status throughout.
  • Date columns are day-first and require dayfirst parsing; default parsing misreads them.
  • Numeric contamination: Amount Paid and Total Sessions Completed contain values that are dates (for example "1903-11-17, 12:00:00"). All numeric fields are coerced with errors="coerce" and rows failing coercion are treated as unusable, not as zero.
  • End Date extends to 2080-09-03 — a dirty sentinel, not a real expiry. Restricting the cohort to End Date before 1 Oct 2026 excludes it.
  • September cohort: 189 paid expiries. 104 renewed, 70 lapsed, 15 frozen. Reconciles exactly.

Limitations — the five that matter most

  • Three fields are excluded for leakage. Days Since Last Visit, Visits in Last 30 Days and Attendance Decline Rate % are computed as at October 2026, after the outcome they would predict. See §6.
  • The logistic model uses 579 of 2,087 records. Four of seven fields are sparsely populated. The model ranks drivers; it is not a member-scoring tool.
  • Renewal is measured on the membership, not the member. A member holding two memberships counts twice. 1,565 of 3,243 Kwality House members in the file hold more than one.
  • Lapsed value is the price paid, not recurring revenue. A ₹175,560 annual lapse and a ₹1,942 single-class lapse are counted at face value; the annualised impact of the two is very different.
  • October 2026 is partial (1–10 October). The win-back result is a floor, not a final figure — some September lapses will yet return.

A note on confidence labels

High confidence means directly measurable in reconciled data with a stable sample. Moderate means the direction is well supported but the magnitude rests on an assumption stated in the text. Low means the finding depends on behaviour the data cannot observe. Every low-confidence item in this report is labelled as such, and none carries more than a quarter of the modelled impact.
